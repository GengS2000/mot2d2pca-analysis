**Table S14. Sensitivity analyses of the association between completed-trajectory turnover and next-bin clinical resolution**

| Analysis | Intervals | Participants | Resolutions | aOR (95% CI) | *P* value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Primary model | 42 | 18 | 18 | 2.75 (1.31–5.78) | 0.008 |
| Preceding bin observed | 38 | 18 | 17 | 2.48 (1.22–5.03) | 0.012 |
| Current activity score >5 | 28 | 15 | 8 | 3.33 (1.24–8.91) | 0.017 |
| Resolution with score decrease ≥2 | 38 | 17 | 14 | 3.15 (1.48–6.70) | 0.003 |
| Medication status unchanged | 34 | 16 | 15 | 6.11 (1.83–20.46) | 0.003 |
| No antibiotics in current or next bin | 29 | 13 | 11 | 1.83 (0.76–4.39) | 0.178 |
| Crohn’s disease only | 36 | 14 | 17 | 3.47 (1.42–8.46) | 0.006 |

*Note.* Each row represents a separately fitted interval-level logistic model. Adjusted odds ratios (aORs) indicate the change in the odds of strict clinical resolution in the next four-week bin per within-analysis standard-deviation increase in completed-trajectory Jensen–Shannon turnover. Each model included current clinical activity score, diagnosis, antibiotic exposure, and immunosuppressant exposure when these covariates varied in the corresponding subset; confidence intervals and *P* values were calculated using participant-clustered robust standard errors. Clinical activity was defined as HBI ≥5 for Crohn’s disease (CD) or SCCAI ≥5 for ulcerative colitis (UC); resolution was defined as HBI ≤4 for CD or SCCAI ≤2 for UC. UC intervals with next-bin SCCAI scores of 3–4 were excluded. In the observed-bin analysis, the bin preceding the current active bin was required to contain an observed metagenomic sample, although turnover remained calculated from the completed trajectories. The score-decrease restriction was applied to resolving intervals, whereas persistently active intervals were retained. Unchanged medication status required both antibiotic and immunosuppressant exposure indicators to remain unchanged between the current and next bins. Turnover and current activity scores were re-standardized within every restricted dataset. These exploratory sensitivity analyses were not adjusted for multiple testing.
